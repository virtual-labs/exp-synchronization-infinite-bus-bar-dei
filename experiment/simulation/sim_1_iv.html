📖 Underlying Principles & Governing Equations

The speed of a DC shunt motor is controlled by inserting external resistance $R_{a_{ext}}$ in the armature circuit. The key relationships are:

  1. Constant Field Current ($I_f$): Because field winding is directly across supply, $I_f = \dfrac{V_t}{R_f}$ remains fixed, thus flux $\phi$ is nearly constant.
  2. Armature Current from Load: Developed torque $T_d = K \cdot I_a$ must equal load torque $T_L$ at steady state ⇒ $I_a = \dfrac{T_L}{K}$.
  3. Back EMF & Speed Equation: Applying KVL to armature loop: $V_t = E_b + I_a (R_{a_{int}} + R_{a_{ext}})$. Since back EMF $E_b = K \cdot \omega$ (mechanical rad/s), solving for speed: $$\omega = \dfrac{V_t - I_a (R_{a_{int}} + R_{a_{ext}})}{K}$$ In RPM: $N = \omega \cdot \dfrac{60}{2\pi}$.
  4. Effect of $R_{a_{ext}}$: As external resistance increases, the voltage drop $I_a R_{a_{ext}}$ rises, reducing back EMF and thereby reducing speed. For a fixed $T_L$, this method provides smooth speed reduction below base speed.
💡 LAB TIP: Armature resistance control is economical for low-power motors and provides smooth torques but is inefficient at low speed due to power loss in the external resistor.
